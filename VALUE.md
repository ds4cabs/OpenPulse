# Why OpenPulse Matters

## The Problem
Adverse drug events kill an estimated 100,000+ Americans per year — more than vehicle accidents. Most trace back to signals that *existed* in post-market data but weren't detected fast enough. The bottleneck isn't access: FDA's FAERS releases roughly 2M reports per year publicly. The bottleneck is the analytical work — running disproportionality metrics, cross-checking labels, controlling for confounders — which is slow and expert-intensive.

## What OpenPulse Does
A drug-event-cohort goes in; PRR / ROR / IC025 signals come out, cross-validated against the current label and external evidence sources. Work that took a pharmacovigilance scientist days now takes minutes, with the same audit trail.

## Who Benefits
- Drug-safety teams at small and mid-cap pharma
- Regulatory affairs at generic manufacturers
- Academic pharmacoepidemiology groups
- Patient-advocacy organizations tracking specific therapies

## Why Now
OpenFDA's FAERS endpoints, structured FDA label data, and modern literature corpora make end-to-end signal detection feasible without licensed databases. LLMs make label-vs-event comparison faster and more accurate than regex-based methods.

## The Bigger Picture
Faster signal detection saves lives at population scale. Every label update issued earlier, every withdrawal warning surfaced earlier, is measured in patient outcomes. Tools that lower the cost of these analyses make pharmacovigilance accessible far beyond the top 20 pharmas.
